N41533 DETECTION STUDY APP, VERSION 2.4.1: RESEARCHER QUICK GUIDE
(9 September 2026)

WHAT ONE SESSION LOOKS LIKE
1. Open jbeim.github.io/flight-study in the headset browser (or double-click
   index.html on the PC for the desktop preview).
2. Home screen -> Start Test Configuration.
3. Setup screen, top to bottom:
   1 Participant code (P01, P02 ...). The code fixes that person's random
     order; the order is shown on the screen and written into every file.
     Then "Enter participant information": code, date and start time are
     filled in for you; researcher initials, venue, headset, certificate,
     hours, vision correction, VR experience and a consent check are
     optional and stay under the code (no names). Researcher, venue and
     headset are remembered from the last session on that headset. You
     can review or edit the same panel on the closing screen.
   2 Choose the 12 clip files from the headset's Movies folder (select all
     twelve at once). Shows 12 green OKs.
   3 Nose calibration: once per headset. Enter VR, look straight at the
     aircraft nose in the video, pull the trigger. Remembered afterward.
   4 Checks: audio files and VR run automatically.
   5 Researcher settings: report e-mail (jbeim@yahoo.com), the Web3Forms
     access key (see E-MAIL below), the viewer seat position (see SEAT
     POSITION below; leave it on "pilot's seat"), and the Short test run
     box (first two approaches only; every file gets _TEST in its name).
   The line under the Begin button tells you exactly what is still missing.
4. Begin participant session: four narration pieces play (welcome and
   safety, briefing, how to respond, ready). Enter the flight -> the
   participant pulls the trigger to start each approach.
5. During an approach: trigger = "I see it" (flight pauses); point and
   trigger again = lock in the direction. Two small panels sit directly
   behind the seat, side by side, a little below eye level (turn right
   round; they are 168 and 192 degrees from the nose and 19 degrees below
   the horizon, out of every window sightline): the red one
   = "pressed by mistake" (the press is kept but marked withdrawn), the
   green one = "replay this approach" (the clip restarts from the start;
   the replay is logged and counted on the card row, and earlier presses
   are kept). Several presses per approach are fine. The flight always
   plays to the end. A break comes after approach 6 (questionnaire), then
   Continue the session.
6. When the twelfth approach ends the CLOSING SCREEN appears. Top part
   is for the participant, bottom part (under the line) is yours.

REPORT AN ISSUE (the amber button at the bottom right of every screen)
Anything odd, at any point: headset or comfort, video or audio,
controller or pointing, procedure or timing, a participant comment,
software, other; with a severity (Note, Minor, Major, Session stopped)
and a sentence in plain words. The app stamps the time, the screen, the
current approach and clip time. Issues go into the event log, a
Pxx_issues.csv file, an Issues sheet in the spreadsheet, an "Issues and
notes" section of the report, three columns of the master-log row
(issues_count, issues_major, issues_text) and STUDY_master_issues.csv.
Notes made on the setup screen before Begin are carried into that
participant's session. The closing screen also has an "Add an issue or
note" button for anything the participant says during the debriefing.
If the headset comes off between approaches or while a pointing is
pending, the resume screen appears and the session continues from the
same point (the pending pointing is kept).

IF THE HEADSET CRASHES, THE BROWSER CLOSES, OR THE PAGE RELOADS
Nothing is lost: every press and every event is written to the browser's
storage as it happens. To carry on:
1. Open the app again. The home screen shows an amber button
   "Continue an interrupted session: P07, approach 7 of 12". Press it.
   (If you do not see it, go to Start Test Configuration and type the
   code; the same amber button appears under the code.)
2. On the setup screen choose the 12 clip files again (the browser cannot
   keep files across a reload; the nose calibration is remembered).
3. Press "Continue this interrupted session at approach N". The
   participant puts the headset on, sees WELCOME BACK, and pulls the
   trigger. An approach that was cut off part-way starts again from its
   beginning; its earlier presses are kept and the restart is logged as a
   replay. Finished approaches are never repeated.
The event log records session_resumed (with the next position, the card
that restarts, the order and its seed) and the report says how many times
the session was resumed. Pressing Begin instead of Continue for a code
that has an unfinished session does NOT overwrite it: the old session is
moved to an archive entry in the browser's storage and a fresh session
starts (the technical log says so).

RESUME SCREEN: RESEARCHER OPTIONS (code required)
Whenever the resume screen is showing (headset off, break, or after a
crash), "Researcher options" at the bottom opens three buttons that need
the researcher code: Restart this approach from the beginning (logged as
a replay), Skip this approach (it is left INCOMPLETE in the report), and
End the session here (goes straight to the closing screen; the approach
in progress is left incomplete and the report can be locked as usual).
Every option writes a researcher_... event with the reason.

SEAT POSITION (researcher settings)
The 360 video is a sphere around the viewer. "Pilot's seat" moves the
viewer's eye point 3% of the sphere radius toward the nose and 1.5%
down, so the instrument panel sits a little closer, the way it does from
the seat. This is not a zoom: things straight ahead are drawn up to 3%
larger, things behind up to 3% smaller, and a drone at 3 or 9 o'clock
keeps its exact size. The setting and its numbers are written to
session_start, the summary, the report text and the master-log row
(seat_offset, seat_offset_fwd, seat_offset_down). "Camera position" turns
the offset off. Use the same setting for every participant.

WHAT ELSE CHANGED IN 2.4 (all verified with the automated suite, 149
checks, and a real-browser check of the 360 view)
- Pointing azimuth is now measured from the calibrated nose. In 2.3 it was
  measured from a fixed direction of the video sphere, which would have
  put every pointing about 79 degrees off; no participant had been run.
- The service panels and the in-flight prompts are placed relative to the
  calibrated nose, so they are truly behind the seat (2.3 would have put
  them over the left wing on the headset).
- Fixed foveated rendering is switched off when VR starts (it blurs the
  edge of the view, where a drone first appears); the value is logged in
  the xr_entered event. The XR reference space is "local" (eye level).
- The order record: session_start, the summary, the text report and the
  master row now carry order_seed (the number the shuffle was seeded
  with), order_method (the sentence describing the shuffle) and
  order_as_presented (the cards that actually ran, in order), alongside
  the planned order and each card's position. Any session's order can be
  regenerated from the code alone; see IRB\Randomization_Method_Note.txt.
- three.js, SheetJS and JSZip are built into index.html: once the page is
  open, nothing else is fetched from the internet (the clips are local;
  the audio and the answers video come from the same site as the page).
- New master-log columns (now 128): order_seed, order_method,
  order_as_presented, seat_offset, seat_offset_fwd, seat_offset_down,
  resumed_after_reload.
- Four optional narration pieces (2.4.1): 14_welcome_back (resume after
  a break, a headset removal or a crash), 15_flight_ready (the moment the
  participant enters the flight), 16_restart_approach (an approach that
  starts again after a crash or a researcher restart), 17_replaying (the
  green panel). Each plays only if its mp3 exists in audio\narration; the
  audio check lists any that are missing but does not block the session.

CLOSING SCREEN, PARTICIPANT PART
  Time in the instrument   shown at the top (headset on to headset off,
                           break included)
  1 Play the debriefing    your recorded debriefing plays; the text is
                           printed under the button so you can read along
  2 Where the drones were  a 68-second video with music: title, then seven
                           seconds for each of the eight drone approaches
                           (the stretch where the drone is most visible, a
                           yellow arrow following its recorded position, a
                           2x close-up inset, its range, and a short label
                           such as "Fixed-wing, orbiting"), then a thank-you
                           card. Test card 2 is shown with the drone drawn
                           in for this video only, with the caption that the
                           participant's own approach was left exactly as
                           recorded; cards 6 and 7 are marked "modeled
                           track". The music is a licensed track (credit on
                           the thank-you card; see Media\Answers_Video_Music).
                           Playing it is logged (answers_video_played).

SESSION CLOCK
A clock runs on the researcher screens (intro, break, closing) from the
moment Enter the flight is pressed until the last approach ends. The
participant row records headset_on, headset_off, minutes_in_instrument
(break included), minutes_break, minutes_approaches_only, and
minutes_session_total (Begin to lock).

MASTER LOG: ONE ROW PER PARTICIPANT
STUDY_master_log.csv (and the Participants sheet of STUDY_master_log.xlsx)
has exactly one row per locked participant, 128 plain columns: code,
date, start time, the participant information, issue counts, timings, hits, misses, false alarms, correct rejections, rates,
d', criterion, press counts, reaction times, pointing error, then for
each card TC01..TC12 its position in the order, outcome, presses,
reaction time, slant range at the hit, and best pointing error. Each
participant's zip also holds that same single row as Pxx_row.csv, so
compiling thirty participants is simply stacking the rows (or opening
the master file, which the app keeps stacking for you on that headset).
Feed STUDY_master_log.csv straight to an analysis tool; the detailed
files (cards, presses, events) stay available for anything deeper.

RESULTS SCREEN (RESEARCHER PART): TWO STEPS
Step 1  Save all report files: one zip lands in the browser's Downloads
        (on the headset: Files app -> Downloads). Contents for participant
        P07, for example:
          P07_row.csv              the one-line master-log row
          P07_report.html          readable report: session summary, one
                                   section per card, one sub-report per press
          P07_report.txt           the same in plain text
          P07_participant_log.xlsx spreadsheet: Summary, Cards, Presses,
                                   Event log sheets
          P07_cards.csv            one row per card (outcome, presses,
                                   reaction time, best pointing error)
          P07_presses.csv          one row per press with the aircraft and
                                   drone geometry at that instant
          P07_events.csv           the raw time-stamped event log
          P07_issues.csv           the issues and notes logged in the session
          P07_results.json         everything, machine-readable
          STUDY_master_log.csv     one row per participant, all locked so far
          STUDY_master_log.xlsx    the same plus card and press sheets
          STUDY_master_cards.csv, STUDY_master_presses.csv   the detail
          STUDY_master_issues.csv  every issue from every locked participant
        Short test runs are kept in the browser but left out of the master
        files, so P00_TEST never lands in the study data.
Step 2  Type the researcher code (the four digits you chose; it is not
        written anywhere in the app's files) and press Lock, process, and
        e-mail. The app rebuilds the reports, saves the zip again, adds the
        participant to the master log, e-mails the report, and locks
        itself. The locked screen stays up until the code is entered
        again; it also offers Re-send, Save the files again, and Save the
        master log.

WHAT THE SCORING MEANS
  HIT                a press on a target card while the drone was in view
                     (window widened by 1 s before and 3 s after)
  MISS               a target card with no hit
  FALSE ALARM        a press on a control card, or a press on a target
                     card outside the drone's window
  CORRECT REJECTION  a control card with no press
  WITHDRAWN          a press the participant flagged with the red panel;
                     it is kept in the log but not counted
  UNSCORED           a press on a target card that has no visibility
                     window (none at present; TC06 and TC07 now use
                     modeled tracks and are scored, flagged modeled)
  Reaction time      press time minus the moment the drone entered view
                     (also given from "first likely visible")
  Pointing error     angle between where the controller pointed (relative
                     to the calibrated nose) and the drone's true direction
                     from the aircraft at the paused instant; split into
                     azimuth and elevation parts
  Geometry per press aircraft position (lat, lon, altitude, height above
                     threshold, ground speed, track, distance and bearing
                     from the threshold, seconds to the threshold) and the
                     drone's position (lat, lon, altitude), slant range,
                     horizontal and vertical separation, clock position,
                     relative bearing and elevation from the aircraft, true
                     bearings both ways, and the drone's distance and
                     bearing from the threshold.
  d'                 detection sensitivity from the hit and false-alarm
                     rates by card, log-linear correction (Hautus, 1995);
                     criterion c is also given.

E-MAIL
A web page cannot send e-mail on its own. The app uses the free Web3Forms
relay: go to web3forms.com once, enter jbeim@yahoo.com, and the site
e-mails you an access key. Paste it into Researcher settings (it is
remembered on that device). At lock the report text and the two CSV
tables go in the body of the e-mail (attachments need a paid plan, so the
zip is the file of record). Only the participant code travels; no names.
Without a key the app still saves everything and opens a mail draft.

DATA SAFETY
Every event is written to the browser's storage as it happens, so a
headset battery dying mid-session loses nothing: reopen the app and use
Continue (see above), or open the stored results under the code. Always
move the zip to the encrypted computer the same day.

KEYBOARD (desktop preview only)
drag = look around, click = trigger, B = the red mistake panel, R = the green replay panel.
